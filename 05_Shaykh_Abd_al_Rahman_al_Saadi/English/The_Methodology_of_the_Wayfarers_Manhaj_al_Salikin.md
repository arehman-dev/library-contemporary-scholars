# The Methodology of the Wayfarers (Manhaj al-Salikin)
### Author: Shaykh Abd al-Rahman ibn Nasir al-Sa'di (1307–1376 AH)
### Translated with Commentary Notes

---

## Author's Introduction

In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

All praise is due to Allah, the Lord of all creation. We praise Him, seek His assistance, and ask for His forgiveness. We send peace and blessings upon our Prophet Muhammad, upon his noble family, and all of his righteous Companions.

To proceed:
This is a concise treatise on the principles of jurisprudence (*Fiqh*), focusing on clarity, authentic textual evidence from the Noble Qur'an and authentic prophetic Sunnah, and eliminating unnecessary scholastic complexity so that the beginner and general believer may grasp the divine ordinances governing their worship, transactions, and daily living.

## Chapter 1: The Book of Purification (Kitab al-Taharah)

Purification is the key to prayer (*Miftah al-Salah*). No prayer is accepted from one who is in a state of ritual impurity until he purifies himself.

### 1. Water and Its Categories
Water is fundamentally pure and purifying. It does not lose its purifying quality unless its color, taste, or odor is altered by an impure substance (*najasah*).
- **Pure Water (*Tahur*)**: Naturally occurring rain, river, sea, well, and snow water.
- **Impure Water (*Najis*)**: Water that has undergone a perceptible change in its taste, smell, or color due to an impure substance falling into it.

### 2. Ritual Impurities (*Najasat*)
The basic ruling regarding all substances is that they are pure unless clear legal evidence establishes their ritual impurity.
Established ritual impurities include:
1. Human urine and excrement.
2. Blood emitted in gushing quantity from slaughtered or living animals (excluding microscopic quantities, fish, and locusts).
3. The meat and by-products of swine and unslaughtered dead animals (*maytah*), except aquatic animals and creatures that have no running blood (e.g., flies, bees).
4. Dog saliva: Which requires seven washings, the first of which is with clean soil or dust.

### 3. The Performance of Ablution (*Wudu*)
The mandatory pillars (*Arkan*) of ablution established by the Qur'anic verse in Surah al-Ma'idah (5:6) are six:
1. Washing the face, which includes rinsing the mouth (*Madmadah*) and inhaling water into the nose (*Istinshaq*).
2. Washing both arms up to and including the elbows.
3. Wiping the entire head, including the ears.
4. Washing both feet up to and including the ankles.
5. Observing the prescribed order (*al-Tartib*).
6. Continuity without excessive delay (*al-Muwalah*).

## Chapter 2: The Maxims of Legal Jurisprudence (*Al-Qawa'id al-Fiqhiyyah*)

The rulings of the Sacred Law revolve around five major universal maxims derived directly from the divine texts:

1. **Matters are judged by their intentions (*Al-Umur bi-Maqasidiha*)**:
   Derived from the statement of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: *"Actions are judged by intentions, and every person will have only what they intended."*

2. **Certainty is not overruled by doubt (*Al-Yaqin la Yazulu bi-al-Shakk*)**:
   A person who is certain of their purification does not consider themselves impure simply because of subsequent suspicion or hesitation.

3. **Hardship begets facility (*Al-Mashaqqah Tajlib al-Taysir*)**:
   All severe conditions (illness, journey, coercion) prompt concessions (*Rukhsah*), such as wiping over leather socks, combining prayers, or performing dry ablution (*Tayammum*).

4. **Harm must be eliminated (*Al-Darar Yuzal*)**:
   Derived from the foundational hadith: *"There shall be no harm and no reciprocation of harm."*

5. **Custom is an arbitrator (*Al-Adah Muhakkamah*)**:
   In matters where the Shari'ah has not fixed an exact linguistic or mathematical definition, common righteous societal custom defines boundaries (such as mutual gift-giving, reasonable financial maintenance, and custody definitions).
